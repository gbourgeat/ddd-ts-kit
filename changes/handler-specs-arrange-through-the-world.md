**The generated handler, query and job specs arrange through the world of their context, and `holdersOf` of `@ddd-ts-kit/auth/testing` gives the actor and the authorizer holding a permission.** Once `test/worlds/<context>.world.ts` exists (`generate test-world <context>`), `generate command`, `generate query` (`--search` too) and `generate job` write an `arrange()` that is one call of the world: `aBillingWorld({ modules: [holders], idPrefix: 'invoice' })` under a container, the buses and the fakes of the world on which the spec registers its handler when wired by hand; the assertions are the same (the answer, the refusal of an actor without the permission, the `it.todo` of the business decision). A spec generated before the world, or when the world lacks what it needs (the fake of the repository of a newer aggregate, an option of a world written by an earlier version), builds the test application or the buses as before, with a one-line comment, and the output of the generator says that `generate test-world <context>` (or `--force`) shortens it; no generator writes a world unasked. `holdersOf(permission, ...more)` answers `{ holder, authorizer }`: a user of the role `holder`, and a `RoleBasedAuthorizer` granting the permissions to that role and to the system actors, so the five lines of every generated spec that built them are one, in both arrangements, and the spec of a job grants its system actor with it. The world of a container takes `fakes` (the fake of a port it cannot build alone, a reader that answers what the spec gives it) and, under tenancy, `tenants`.

To move: run `generate test-world <context>` first (with `--force` on a world written by an earlier version, so that it takes `fakes` and `tenants`), then regenerate a spec with `--force` to get the new arrangement, after committing its tests: `--force` writes the files of the use case again. Specs left as they are keep passing.
