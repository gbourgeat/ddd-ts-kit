**A criteria searches a text whatever its case: `containsIgnoringCase` and `startsWithIgnoringCase`, on every store.** A field of type `string` declares them beside `contains` and `startsWith` (`filter: ['containsIgnoringCase']`), and `where('name', 'containsIgnoringCase', 'dupont')` then finds `Dupont` and `DUPONT`. The text is taken as literally as `contains` takes it (`%`, `_` and `\` are characters, an empty text is refused), each letter of both sides is put in lower case and nothing else is folded: an accent is kept, so `e` does not find `é`. In memory it is `foldCase` of `@ddd-ts-kit/kernel` (`toLowerCase` of JavaScript); `@ddd-ts-kit/knex`, `@ddd-ts-kit/drizzle` and `@ddd-ts-kit/typeorm` write an `ILIKE`, `@ddd-ts-kit/prisma` the `insensitive` mode, `@ddd-ts-kit/opensearch` a `regexp` query that names both forms of each letter. `criteriaContract` of `@ddd-ts-kit/testing` holds them to the same rows for the ASCII letters and for a letter with one upper-case and one lower-case form (`É` and `é`); outside them (the two `I` of Turkish, `ß`, the final sigma) each store follows its own table, and a PostgreSQL database created with the `C` locale folds the ASCII letters alone. A route that reads its criteria with `criteriaQuerySchema` of `@ddd-ts-kit/http` takes `filter[name]=containsIgnoringCase:dupont` once the definition declares the operator. No b-tree index serves an `ILIKE`: on PostgreSQL a GIN index of `pg_trgm` on the column does, in a migration of the project. The rule and its limits are in "What a criteria means" of `packages/kernel/README.md`.
