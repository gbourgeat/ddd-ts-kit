**Breaking: `ddd-ts-kit check` reports an adapter the default policy cuts before the slower adapter it calls, rule `aspects/timeout-covers-delegate`.** A new rule, an error at once. It reports one case, the one nobody chose: an `external` or `gateway` adapter left to the default policy of the platform (no `ownPolicy(...)`, no `timeout(...)` of its own; an `@Adapter` that names no kind is `external`) that is given a port, a constructor parameter typed by it, whose adapter states a longer `timeout(<ms>)` around all its methods. Every call the inner adapter would still answer then fails in the outer one first, at the default limit, as a timeout of the outer one. The default limit is the fallback of `OUTBOUND_TIMEOUT_MS` in the configuration of the platform, else `timeoutMs` of `resilience/default-resilience-settings.ts`. The rule reads `@Adapter`, `@Gateway` and the bindings of the container of the kit (`bind(Port).toClass(Adapter).as(kind).with(...)`). An adapter that states its own limit, even a shorter one, chose it and is never reported. The fix is `ownPolicy(timeout(<ms>))` on the outer adapter, or `ownPolicy()` alone when it only hands the call over. Not judged, because the text does not show it: a platform wired by hand (`platform.adapter(...)`); a duration or a kind that is not a literal; an aspect that is a variable, a spread, a call of another name or under `when(...)`; a `timeout(` nested in the options of another aspect; a `@Timeout` on a method; a port or an adapter bound more than once, bound again by `rebind(`, or bound under a condition; two ports of one name unless each file imports its own from the file that declares it; an adapter reached through a middle adapter; a default limit that is not one number, or an environment that sets `OUTBOUND_TIMEOUT_MS`; the retries. See "A delegating adapter" of `docs/concepts/resilience.md`.

To move: `npx ddd-ts-kit check --rule aspects/timeout-covers-delegate` lists the adapters to take up; each gets the policy the fix names.
