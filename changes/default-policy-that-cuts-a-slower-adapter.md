**`ddd-ts-kit check` reports an adapter that is cut before the slower adapter it calls: rule `aspects/timeout-covers-delegate`.** Breaking: a new rule, an error at once. It reads the adapters a project declares (`@Adapter`, `@Gateway`) or binds with the container of the kit (`bind(Port).toClass(Adapter).as(kind).with(...)`), the classes that type the constructor of each, and the durations written as numbers. An adapter that is given a port, and whose time limit is shorter than the limit the adapter bound to that port states for itself, is reported with the two durations: every call the inner adapter would still answer fails in the outer one first, as a timeout of the outer one. The limit of an adapter is the `timeout(<ms>)` of its declaration or binding and, when its kind is `external` or `gateway` (an `@Adapter` that names no kind is `external`) and it states no `ownPolicy(...)`, the default of the platform: the fallback of `OUTBOUND_TIMEOUT_MS` in the configuration of the platform, else `timeoutMs` of `resilience/default-resilience-settings.ts`. The adapter behind the port also counts the `@Timeout` of its methods. The fix is `ownPolicy(timeout(<ms>))` on the outer adapter, at least as long as the inner one, or `ownPolicy()` alone when it only hands the call over. What the text does not show is not judged: a duration or a kind read from the configuration or a variable, an aspect under `when(...)`, a `@Timeout` on a method of the outer adapter, an environment that sets `OUTBOUND_TIMEOUT_MS`, a platform wired by hand and the retries. See "A delegating adapter" of `docs/concepts/resilience.md`.

To move: `npx ddd-ts-kit check --rule aspects/timeout-covers-delegate` lists the adapters to take up; each gets the policy the fix names.
