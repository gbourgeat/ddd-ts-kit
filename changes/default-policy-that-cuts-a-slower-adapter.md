**A default time limit that cuts a slower adapter behind it says so, and `ddd-ts-kit check` reports it before it runs: rule `aspects/timeout-covers-delegate`.** The platform of a generated project puts a default policy around every `external` or `gateway` adapter that states none: 2 seconds per attempt. An adapter that only hands its call to another adapter, slow by nature and bound with a longer limit of its own (a remote reader with 120 seconds), failed every call at 2 seconds, and the error named the outer adapter and nothing else. Two things change. `timeout(ms, { statedBy })` of `@ddd-ts-kit/aspects` takes who states the limit: the `TimeoutError` keeps it in `statedBy` and ends its message with it, its code `TIMEOUT` and its `timeoutMs` unchanged; the `default-policy.ts` that `init` writes names itself there, so the message reads `RemoteQuoteReader.read did not answer within 2000 ms (the default policy of the external and gateway adapters, OUTBOUND_TIMEOUT_MS: ownPolicy(timeout(<ms>)) on the declaration or the binding of this adapter states another, at least as long as the adapter it calls)`. And the new rule `aspects/timeout-covers-delegate`, an error, reads the adapters a project declares (`@Adapter`, `@Gateway`) or binds with the container of the kit, the classes that type the constructor of each, the `@Timeout` of the methods and the durations written as numbers: an adapter whose limit (its own, else the default of the platform for an `external` or `gateway` one) is shorter than the limit the adapter behind one of its ports states for itself is reported, with the two durations. The fix is `ownPolicy(timeout(<ms>))` on the outer adapter, at least as long as the inner one, or `ownPolicy()` alone when it only hands the call over. A duration read from the configuration, an environment that sets `OUTBOUND_TIMEOUT_MS`, a platform wired by hand and the retries are not judged: see "A delegating adapter" of `docs/concepts/resilience.md`.

To move: `npx ddd-ts-kit check --rule aspects/timeout-covers-delegate` lists the adapters to take up; each gets the policy the fix names. A project generated earlier takes the message by adding `{ statedBy: '<who states it>' }` to the `timeout(settings.timeoutMs)` of its `src/platform/resilience/default-policy.ts`, as the template of this version does; nothing else changes, and a test that compares the whole message of a `TimeoutError` of the default policy reads the new ending.
